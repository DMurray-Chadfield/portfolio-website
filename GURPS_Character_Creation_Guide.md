# GURPS 4th Edition: Character Creation Process & Point Spending Guide

This document provides a comprehensive guide to creating a character in **GURPS Fourth Edition** (Generic Universal RolePlaying System), based on *GURPS Basic Set: Characters*. It details the step-by-step creation process and explains all options for point spending, including basic attributes, secondary characteristics, social background, wealth, advantages, disadvantages, perks, and quirks.

---

## 1. Overview of Character Creation

In GURPS, character creation is point-based. Rather than picking a class, players are given a budget of **character points** by the Game Master (GM) to build a hero from the ground up.

* **Starting Points:** Determines the campaign's power level.
  * **Feeble / Children:** Under 25 points
  * **Average / Ordinary Citizens:** 25–50 points
  * **Competent / Experienced:** 75–100 points
  * **Heroic / Career Adventurers:** 100–200 points (Typical baseline: 150 points)
  * **Larger-Than-Life / Epic:** 200–300 points
  * **Superhuman / High Fantasy:** 300–500 points
  * **Godlike:** 1,000+ points
* **Disadvantage Limit:** To prevent dysfunctional characters, GMs usually cap points gained from disadvantages. A common benchmark is a maximum of **50% of starting points** (e.g., -75 points in a 150-point game). Campaign-mandated disadvantages and racial traits do not count against this cap.

### Step-by-Step Creation Checklist
1. **Character Concept:** Define who the character is, their background, occupation, and role in the group.
2. **Basic Attributes & Secondary Characteristics:** Purchase core physical and mental scores.
3. **Build & Physical Appearance:** Determine height, weight, age, and appearance levels.
4. **Social Background:** Establish Technology Level (TL), Cultural Familiarity, and Languages.
5. **Wealth & Social Standing:** Determine starting money, Status, Rank, and privileges.
6. **Advantages & Perks:** Buy special abilities, talents, and social edges.
7. **Disadvantages & Quirks:** Select flaws, handicaps, and personality quirks to gain extra points.
8. **Skills & Techniques:** Spend points on trained abilities, trade skills, and combat proficiencies.

## 2. Basic Attributes & Secondary Characteristics

Attributes form the core framework of every character. An average human score is **10** (costs 0 points); each level above or below 10 costs or refunds the listed points.

### Basic Attributes

| Attribute | Full Name | Cost per Level (relative to 10) | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ST** | Strength | $\pm10$ pts/level | Physical muscle, bulk, lifting capacity, basic damage, and Hit Points. |
| **DX** | Dexterity | $\pm20$ pts/level | Agility, coordination, motor skills, athletic abilities, and combat skills. |
| **IQ** | Intelligence | $\pm20$ pts/level | Brainpower, creativity, memory, perception, reasoning, and mental skills. |
| **HT** | Health | $\pm10$ pts/level | Stamina, physical grit, resistance to poison/disease, and energy reserve. |

*Attribute Rating Scale:* 6 or less = Crippling; 7 = Poor; 8–9 = Below Average; 10 = Average; 11–12 = Above Average; 13–14 = Exceptional; 15+ = Amazing.

---

### Secondary Characteristics

Secondary characteristics derive directly from basic attributes but can be modified independently using character points.

| Characteristic          | Base Value              | Cost to Modify             | Description                                                           |
| :---------------------- | :---------------------- | :------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Damage (Dmg)**        | Derived from ST         | N/A (via ST)               | Basic thrusting (`thr`) and swinging (`sw`) damage dice.              |
| **Basic Lift (BL)**     | $(ST \times ST) / 5$ lbs | Modified via Lifting ST    | Maximum weight lifted overhead with one hand in one second.           |
| **Hit Points (HP)**     | Equal to ST             | $\pm2$ pts per $\pm1$ HP   | Body's capacity to sustain physical injury.                           |
| **Will**                | Equal to IQ             | $\pm5$ pts per $\pm1$ Will | Psychological stress resistance and mental fortitude.                 |
| **Perception (Per)**    | Equal to IQ             | $\pm5$ pts per $\pm1$ Per  | General alertness, sensory awareness, and Sense rolls.                |
| **Fatigue Points (FP)** | Equal to HT             | $\pm3$ pts per $\pm1$ FP   | Energy reserve lost to exertion, starvation, or spellcasting.         |
| **Basic Speed**         | $(DX + HT) / 4$         | $\pm5$ pts per $\pm0.25$   | Reflexes, initiative order, and base Dodge score ($Basic Speed + 3$). |
| **Basic Move**          | $\lfloor \text{Basic Speed} \rfloor$ | $\pm5$ pts per $\pm1$ yd/s | Ground movement speed in yards per second (unencumbered). |

## 3. Social Background, Wealth & Influence

### Technology Level (TL)
Measures technological access. Personal TL matches campaign TL by default (0 points).
* **Low TL:** $-5$ points per TL below campaign average.
* **High TL:** $+5$ points per TL above campaign average.

### Cultural Familiarity & Languages
* **Cultural Familiarity:** 1 point per familiar human culture; 2 points per alien culture. Eliminates the $-3$ penalty when interacting socially.
* **Languages:** Native language (Spoken/Written) is free. Additional languages cost points based on comprehension:
  * **Broken:** 1 pt spoken / 1 pt written (2 pts total). $-3$ to language-dependent skills.
  * **Accented:** 2 pts spoken / 2 pts written (4 pts total). $-1$ to language-dependent skills; obvious foreign accent.
  * **Native:** 3 pts spoken / 3 pts written (6 pts total). Complete fluency and idiomatic mastery.

---

### Wealth & Financial Status

Starting wealth depends on campaign Tech Level (e.g., TL3 = \$1,000; TL8 = \$20,000).

| Level | Cost | Starting Wealth Modifier |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dead Broke** | $-25$ pts | \$0 starting wealth; no income or property. |
| **Poor** | $-15$ pts | $1/5$ average starting wealth. |
| **Struggling** | $-10$ pts | $1/2$ average starting wealth. |
| **Average** | $0$ pts | Standard starting wealth for the campaign TL. |
| **Comfortable** | $+10$ pts | $2 \times$ average starting wealth. |
| **Wealthy** | $+20$ pts | $5 \times$ average starting wealth. |
| **Very Wealthy** | $+30$ pts | $20 \times$ average starting wealth. |
| **Filthy Rich** | $+50$ pts | $100 \times$ average starting wealth. |
| **Multimillionaire** | $+50$ pts (Level 1), $+25$ pts/extra level | $1,000 \times$ average (Level 1), $10,000 \times$ average (Level 2), etc. |

* **Independent Income (1 pt/level):** Unearned monthly income equal to $1\%$ of starting wealth per level.
* **Debt ($-1$ pt/level):** Monthly obligation requiring $1\%$ of starting wealth per level.

---

### Influence & Social Rank

* **Status (5 pts/level):** Measures social standing and authority (typically ranges from $-2$ to $+8$). Higher status gives a $+1$ reaction bonus per level difference from social inferiors.
* **Rank (5 or 10 pts/level):** Authority within specific institutions (Administrative, Merchant, Military, Police, Religious).
  * $5$ pts/level if coexisting with Status.
  * $10$ pts/level if replacing Status entirely.
* **Reputation (Variable):** Public notoriety. Modifies reaction rolls by $\pm1$ per 5 base points. Modified by group size affected ($\times1/3$ to $\times1$) and frequency of recognition ($\times1/3$ to $\times1$).

## 4. Point Spending Options: Advantages

Advantages are beneficial mental, physical, or social traits giving characters an edge.

### Advantage Categories
* **Mental ($\psi$):** Originates from the mind or soul.
* **Physical ($\mathbf{P}$):** Biological/physical body traits.
* **Social ($\mathbf{S}$):** Associated with identity, status, or position.
* **Exotic ($\mathbf{E}$):** Non-human physical/biological capabilities.
* **Supernatural ($\mathbf{Su}$):** Magic, divine, or psionic abilities defying natural law.
* **Mundane:** Ordinary edges available to human characters.

### Perks (1 Point Each)
* **Sample Perks:**
  * **Alcohol Tolerance** [1] *(Physical)* — +2 on HT rolls to resist drunkenness.
  * **Honest Face** [1] *(Physical)* — Looks naturally innocent; +1 to Acting to claim innocence.

## 5. Point Spending Options: Disadvantages

Disadvantages are handicaps, flaws, or vulnerabilities that give the character **back character points** to spend elsewhere.

### Disadvantage Categories
* **Mental ($\psi$):** Mind-based flaws, psychological compulsions, or mental conditions.
* **Physical ($\mathbf{P}$):** Physical injuries, illnesses, or anatomical handicaps.
* **Social ($\mathbf{S}$):** Negative standing, legal troubles, or poor social standing.
* **Mundane / Exotic / Supernatural:** Identical origin definitions as advantages.

---

### Self-Control Roll (CR) for Mental Disadvantages
Many mental disadvantages represent compulsions or psychological flaws that can be resisted in stressful situations. When triggered, the character must roll **3d against their Will**:
* **Standard Self-Control Roll (CR 12):** Listed point value.
* **Severe Compulsion (CR 9):** Multiply point value by **$\times1.5$**.
* **Resistless Compulsion (CR 6):** Multiply point value by **$\times2.0$**.
* **Easy to Resist (CR 15):** Multiply point value by **$\times0.5$**.

---

### Detailed Disadvantage List

* **Sample Disadvantages:**
  * **Bad Temper** [-10] *(Mental)* — Must roll self-control (CR 12) whenever insulted or frustrated, or fly into a rage.
  * **Blindness** [-50] *(Physical)* — Complete loss of vision; -10 to combat actions.
  * **Code of Honor** [-5 to -15] *(Mental)* — Self-imposed binding rules of conduct.

---

### Quirks (1 Point Gain Each)
* **Sample Quirks:**
  * **Attitude Quirk** [-1] *(Mental)* — Minor bias or personal dislike (e.g., "Dislikes dogs").
  * **Physical Quirk** [-1] *(Physical)* — Trivial cosmetic feature (e.g., "Unusual bright green eyes").

## 6. Point Spending Options: Skills & Techniques

Skills represent trained knowledge, physical techniques, or learned expertise.

### Skill Difficulty & Attribute Baselines
Skills are based on a core attribute (**DX**, **IQ**, **HT**, or **ST**) and categorized by difficulty: **Easy (E)**, **Average (A)**, **Hard (H)**, and **Very Hard (VH)**.

#### Skill Cost Table

| Desired Skill Level Relative to Attribute | Easy (E) | Average (A) | Hard (H) | Very Hard (VH) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Attribute $- 2$** | — | — | — | 1 pt |
| **Attribute $- 1$** | — | 1 pt | 1 pt | 2 pts |
| **Attribute $+ 0$** | 1 pt | 2 pts | 4 pts | 8 pts |
| **Attribute $+ 1$** | 2 pts | 4 pts | 8 pts | 12 pts |
| **Attribute $+ 2$** | 4 pts | 8 pts | 12 pts | 16 pts |
| **Attribute $+ 3$** | 8 pts | 12 pts | 16 pts | 20 pts |
| **$+1$ Level Beyond** | $+4$ pts | $+4$ pts | $+4$ pts | $+4$ pts |

* **Defaults:** Unlearned skills can often be rolled against an attribute default (e.g., DX$-5$ for Broadsword) or a related skill default.
* **Techniques:** Specialized combat maneuvers or maneuvers within a skill (e.g., Disarm, Feint, Targeted Attack) bought up independently using the Technique Cost Table.

---

## Summary of Character Point Budget Formula

$$\text{Total Character Points} = \text{Starting Base Points} + \text{Points from Disadvantages} + \text{Points from Quirks}$$

$$\text{Spent Points} = \text{Attributes} + \text{Secondary Characteristics} + \text{Social Background (TL, Culture, Languages, Wealth, Status, Rank, Reputation)} + \text{Advantages} + \text{Perks} + \text{Skills} + \text{Techniques}$$

To finalize creation, **Spent Points** must equal **Total Character Points**.
